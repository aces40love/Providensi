The route from idea to decision

Four stages.
No automatic leap to production.

A proposed engagement should make its deliverables and decision points explicit. Each next stage depends on the bank’s review of the prior result.

01 / Define

Put the assignment in writing.

Choose a workflow, approved inputs, a tangible deliverable, and the bank roles that will sponsor and review it.

Discussion output
A scope brief with excluded actions, open questions, and the requirements to resolve.
Bank decision
Is this a useful, appropriately bounded candidate for evaluation?

02 / Establish

Agree on the operating boundary.

Review the proposed configuration, information paths, access, evidence, responsibilities, and commercial terms.

Required before testing
A documented scope, applicable safeguards, test plan, acceptance criteria, and cost assumptions.
Bank decision
Are the evidence and arrangements sufficient for the proposed test?

03 / Evaluate

Test the whole workflow.

Use representative approved examples. Include incorrect answers, missing records, denied access, interruption, and manual fallback.

Evaluation output
Results, exceptions, reviewer corrections, staff effort, and operating costs.
Bank decision
Stop, revise, or consider a defined expansion.

04 / Operate, if approved

Make ongoing ownership explicit.

Establish the production scope, support, changes, records, recovery, monitoring, and an exit path before introducing broader use.

Operating output
Approved responsibilities and runbooks, appropriate contractual commitments, and continuing review.
Bank decision
Who authorizes production use and subsequent material changes?

This describes a proposed engagement structure, not a completed implementation, a fixed delivery schedule, or an automatic approval process.

Fit the bank, not the other way around

Your hardware is
one part of the answer.

Explore a dedicated deployment on infrastructure your bank controls. Confirm supported hardware, operating environments, identity requirements, capacity, and dependencies for the proposed arrangement.

Do not assume a desktop feature is included, fully local, or compatible with your core system. The exact capabilities belong in the scope.

Map the information path →
Workload and capacity
Document volumes, file types, simultaneous users, response expectations, growth, and representative testing.
Access and approved sources
Source ownership, user entitlements, administrative roles, support access, and changes to permissions.
Operating responsibility
Patches, releases, testing after changes, backup, restore, incident response, and the manual alternative.
Rights and exit
Software licensing, rights to bank data and custom deliverables, export, retention, deletion, and transition responsibilities.

Bank-hosted does not automatically mean disconnected. Any external processing or service dependency must be identified and agreed for the proposed workflow.

Price the operating arrangement

A bank deployment is
not a desktop subscription.

A dedicated engagement is scoped and quoted separately. A useful proposal makes the one-time work, recurring cost, bank effort, and exit assumptions visible.

Cost areaWhat to include in the discussionHow to compare
EnvironmentHardware or hosting, storage, security tooling, backup, and capacity.Initial cost plus ongoing operation and replacement assumptions.
ImplementationConfiguration, source preparation, identity work, permitted integrations, and training.Defined deliverables, responsible parties, and acceptance conditions.
EvaluationTest cases, reviewer effort, corrections, independent review where appropriate, and failed attempts.The complete cost of an accepted result.
Ongoing operationLicensing, support, monitoring, updates, re-evaluation, and recovery exercises.Recurring vendor cost plus the bank’s internal staff effort.
Change and exitNew workflows, material changes, data export, transition, and retirement.What is included, what is separately priced, and what the bank keeps.

No bank-deployment price or service level is implied by this proposal. Desktop plans and their usage allowances do not price a dedicated bank environment.

Measure the complete job

A faster draft is
only part of the result.

Compare the same unit of work before and during the pilot. Do not leave reviewer time, failed attempts, and corrections out of the calculation.

Quality

Source accuracy, missed exceptions, unsupported conclusions, extraction errors, and the severity of each failure.

Effort

Preparation, review, correction, administration, and the manual work needed when the workflow does not succeed.

Operating value

Useful capacity, turnaround, customer-service opportunities, total cost, and any cash savings actually realized.
