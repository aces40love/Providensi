STORY 17 / Truthful marketing

A fictional customer cannot become a real testimonial.

The FTC’s reviews and testimonials rule applies to materially false representations, including AI-generated endorsements presented as genuine experiences.

Providensi insightsOctober 2, 2026Sources linked below
Regulation and business guidanceRepresentation and conduct determine the issueRule effective October 21, 2024

This is not a categorical ban on AI avatars or clearly fictional creative work. The rule addresses specified false representations and conduct, with applicable knowledge standards.

A generation feature is not legal permission

The FTC’s rule addresses specified false consumer reviews and consumer or celebrity testimonials. Section 465.2 includes material misrepresentations about whether a person exists, used the product or service, or had the described experience.

An AI-generated fictional reviewer presented as a genuine satisfied customer can create exposure for the business using it. The rule’s application depends on the representation and conduct, and knowledge standards vary. AI avatars themselves are not categorically prohibited.

Let the evidence carry the message

Create strong marketing from approved product facts, genuine customer experiences, and clearly labeled illustrations. Keep permission, substantiation, and required disclosures with the campaign before anyone authorizes publication.

  • Do not invent customer identities, outcomes, quotes, or endorsements.
  • Document consent and substantiation for genuine testimonials.
  • Review claims and disclosures before publishing or buying ads.
