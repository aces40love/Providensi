STORY 09 / Research & evidence

A convincing citation still needs to be checked.

The Tow Center’s 2025 AI-search research is a useful starting point for a better habit: open the source and confirm that it supports the claim.

Providensi insightsOctober 2, 2026Sources linked below
Bounded news-source benchmarkHistorical study, not a current accuracy scoreReport published March 6, 2025

The study concerned identifying news articles from excerpts. It is not a measure of every AI answer or present-day versions. Incorrect identification, fabricated links, and refusals are different outcomes.

What the research asks buyers to notice

The Tow Center’s March 2025 report examined AI search tools on a specific source-identification task involving news excerpts. The report and its coverage described problems with correct identification and citations. The findings are a reason to inspect evidence, not a universal error rate for all AI work.

A link can exist without supporting the sentence beside it. A recognizable publisher can be cited for the wrong article. A refusal to answer is different from confidently inventing a reference. A meaningful review preserves those distinctions.

Evidence should be usable, not decorative

For an important business claim, ask for the supporting passage, source date, and a clear explanation of what the passage does and does not establish. Treat unavailable evidence as a gap to resolve, not permission to fill the space with confident language.

  • Open the source for a material factual claim.
  • Separate quotations, supported facts, inference, and uncertainty.
  • Check current information before using an older source for a current decision.
