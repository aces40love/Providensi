STORY 07 / Enterprise due diligence

An enterprise label does not answer every retention question.

Model-specific retention policies show why procurement teams need the exact service, workspace, exceptions, and agreement, not a broad enterprise promise.

Providensi insightsOctober 2, 2026Sources linked below
Model-specific commercial policyDefined scope and eligibility exceptionsPolicy effective June 9, 2026

The covered-model policy does not cancel every zero-data-retention arrangement. Other models and certain eligible organizations remain subject to different terms. Safety retention does not itself grant training permission.

The detail lives below the enterprise headline

Anthropic’s covered-model policy describes 30-day retention for prompts and outputs used with designated models. Its scope includes certain organizations otherwise configured for zero data retention, with model, workspace, platform, and eligibility distinctions.

The policy says other models remain under their existing terms and identifies exceptions for eligible organizations notified separately. It also describes controlled review and deletion, with safety and legal exceptions. These details matter more than a general label such as enterprise.

Buy the arrangement you actually need

A security review should identify the model or service being used, the workspace configuration, the data location, the retention trigger, and the governing contract. A promise that applies to one configuration should not silently become an assumption about another.

  • Name the exact service and deployment configuration in your requirements.
  • Ask whether policy updates or model changes alter retention obligations.
  • Keep the decision, exceptions, and contract references available to the team.
