STORY 02 / Agent security

The document was supposed to be a source, not the boss.

GeminiJack demonstrated how malicious instructions in connected content could redirect an enterprise AI workflow. The reported vulnerability was remediated.

Providensi insightsOctober 2, 2026Sources linked below
Security research demonstrationReported remediatedPublic disclosure December 2025

The attack required relevant connections, accessible malicious content, and a query that retrieved it. The sources do not establish criminal exploitation, exposure of every Gemini product, or an ongoing vulnerability.

What the researchers demonstrated

Noma’s GeminiJack research examined enterprise retrieval workflows involving Gemini Enterprise and Vertex AI Search. Malicious instructions placed in connected content could influence the assistant after a normal query caused that content to be retrieved.

The demonstration reportedly led to information disclosure through an external request. Receiving an email by itself was not sufficient. Noma and independent reporting described corrective updates, without establishing an exact completed deployment date.

More sources also mean more untrusted material

An assistant may need to read a page, document, or message without accepting its instructions. When it also has tools, the consequences of confusion can reach beyond a bad answer. Decide which actions the assignment needs and restrict unnecessary authority.

  • Treat retrieved content as evidence to assess, not permission to expand the task.
  • Separate research from sending, publishing, or changing business records.
  • Inspect recipients and destinations before authorizing consequential actions.
