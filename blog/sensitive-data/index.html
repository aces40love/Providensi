STORY 01 / Information privacy

A confidential label is not the whole protection plan.

A resolved Microsoft Copilot defect shows why teams should test what connected AI can actually process, not rely on a reassuring label alone.

Providensi insightsOctober 2, 2026Sources linked below
Deployed product defectReported resolvedReported February 2026

The reports concern processing of confidential email despite configured protections. They do not establish outside theft, cross-tenant disclosure, or model-training use.

What happened

In February 2026, reporting attributed to Microsoft an acknowledgment that Copilot could process and summarize certain confidential email despite data-protection policies. The associated NHSmail advisory now identifies the issue as resolved.

This distinction matters: inappropriate processing is a serious control issue, but it is not automatically evidence that messages were stolen or disclosed to another organization. The lesson is to examine the actual operation and the specific safeguard that failed.

What a business can learn

A classification label, permission setting, and contractual promise answer different questions. Before connecting a live mailbox, identify the allowed folders, data categories, and actions. Evaluate a representative task with synthetic messages and inspect the result.

  • Separate what an account can access from what a particular assignment is allowed to use.
  • Test exclusions with fictional information before relying on them for sensitive work.
  • Review the service agreement and report unexpected processing rather than assuming labels are sufficient.

A PRACTICAL THADDEUS WORKFLOW

Define the access before assigning the work.

Use a plan to name approved inputs, permitted actions, and review criteria. Connect only the accounts the assignment needs, then inspect the deliverable and the information it contains. This gives your team a concrete process to evaluate rather than a blanket privacy promise.
