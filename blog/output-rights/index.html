STORY 15 / Output ownership & human authorship

Permission to use an asset is not the same as owning exclusive rights.

The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes AI assistance from the human authorship needed for copyright protection. That distinction matters to every content business.

Providensi insightsOctober 2, 2026Sources linked below
Official copyright guidanceHuman contribution and the particular work matterCopyright Office announcement January 29, 2025

This concerns U.S. copyrightability, not a universal answer about infringement, trademarks, contracts, or other jurisdictions. A particular asset needs its own assessment.

What the guidance distinguishes

The U.S. Copyright Office says AI-assisted work can qualify for copyright when a human determines sufficient expressive elements. Human expression, creative selection and arrangement, and modifications can matter. Merely supplying prompts is not sufficient by itself.

A provider may permit commercial use under its terms without creating the exclusivity a buyer expects from copyright. Those are different legal questions, and removing a watermark does not answer either of them.

Make human authorship part of the process

For an important business asset, preserve the original brief, human-authored components, meaningful edits, and design decisions. This is useful project practice and can support a later rights review, without determining the legal outcome.

  • Identify the human-created expression and substantive revisions.
  • Retain applicable licenses and source permissions.
  • Obtain qualified advice before relying on exclusivity for a high-value asset.
