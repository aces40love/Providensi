STORY 16 / Employment & accountable decisions

Buying hiring software does not outsource responsibility.

The Mobley v. Workday proceedings illustrate the legal questions around AI-assisted screening. Allegations and procedural rulings are not findings of discrimination.

Providensi insightsOctober 2, 2026Sources linked below
Contested litigationProcedural decisions, not a merits findingProcedural rulings May 2025 and June 2026

Workday disputes the allegations. Preliminary collective certification and surviving a dismissal motion do not establish wrongdoing, a present-day bias rate, or the complete current case outcome.

What the record establishes

The Civil Rights Litigation Clearinghouse records preliminary certification of an age-discrimination collective in Mobley v. Workday on May 16, 2025. Its document index also lists a June 22, 2026 order granting in part and denying in part a dismissal motion.

These developments concern allegations about applicant screening. They are not a finding that the software discriminated, and Workday contests the allegations. The lesson for a buyer is about responsibility and evaluation, not a shortcut to declaring a vendor guilty.

Keep consequential employment decisions human-led

Organizations need processes that account for relevant law, accessibility, validation, and review. An AI-generated rationale can look orderly without establishing that the underlying decision is fair, lawful, or appropriate.

  • Define the limited administrative task the tool is allowed to perform.
  • Review job-related criteria and accessibility with qualified people.
  • Do not infer sensitive characteristics or delegate final hiring decisions to an unsupervised assistant.
