STORY 13 / Human relationships & responsibility

Useful support should keep people connected to people.

Research on emotional dependence raises questions about how people use chatbots. The findings require care: association is not proof of causation.

Providensi insightsOctober 2, 2026Sources linked below
Experiment with observational analysesAssociation, not a proven causal effect2025 research paper

Interaction conditions were randomized, but voluntary usage duration was not. The study did not compare chatbot use with no chatbot use, and assigned conditions did not show significant overall effects on the principal outcomes.

What the study can and cannot tell us

A four-week study with 981 completing participants examined chatbot interaction modes and conversation types, alongside patterns of voluntary use. The paper reports associations between heavier use and worse outcomes on measures including emotional dependence and problematic use.

That does not establish that longer use caused those outcomes. Usage duration was not randomized, and there was no non-chatbot comparison group. The assigned experimental conditions also did not show significant overall effects on the main outcomes. The uncertainty belongs beside the finding.

Design the work around people and outcomes

For a business, an assistant should help people accomplish defined work and collaborate effectively, not become the only place they seek judgment or support. Clear tasks, human review, and real team relationships remain valuable.

  • Set a useful outcome rather than equating more conversation with more value.
  • Keep consequential decisions connected to accountable people.
  • Avoid presenting a conversational product as a substitute for personal or professional support.
